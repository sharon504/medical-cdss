Pulmonary tuberculosis: clinical reference summary

Findings. Pulmonary tuberculosis (PTB) presents classically with more than two weeks of productive cough, night sweats, low-grade evening fever, weight loss, anorexia, and hemoptysis in advanced disease. Host risk factors include immunosuppression, diabetes, malnutrition, and close TB contact. In India,           
where the project context is set, universal drug susceptibility testing at diagnosis is recommended practice.

Differential diagnosis (ranked): (1) pulmonary tuberculosis; (2) community-acquired bacterial pneumonia; (3) lung malignancy, especially in older smokers; (4) fungal infections (histoplasmosis, aspergillosis); (5) sarcoidosis; (6) bronchiectasis.

Recommended investigations. Sputum smear for acid-fast bacilli plus Xpert MTB/RIF (NAAT) is the recommended first diagnostic test; chest radiograph with cavitation, upper-lobe infiltrates, and hilar lymphadenopathy; tuberculin skin test or IGRA for latent infection assessment; HIV test for all; CBC, LFTs before therapy. Bronchoscopy for sputum-negative cases with high suspicion.

Public health note: PTB is a notifiable disease in India; diagnosis triggers contact tracing and standardized treatment under national program supervision. The CDSS must cite notified-guideline retrieval passages for any therapeutic statement.
