Hyperthyroidism (Graves disease): clinical reference summary

Findings. Hyperthyroidism presents with weight loss despite normal appetite, heat intolerance, palpitations, tremor, anxiety, insomnia, sweating, and fatigue. Graves disease adds diffuse smooth goiter, ophthalmopathy (proptosis, diplopia), and pretibial myxedema; brisk reflexes and wide pulse pressure are common examination findings. Thyrotoxic crisis is a life-threatening decompensation with fever and delirium.

Differential diagnosis (ranked): (1) Graves disease, suppressed TSH with elevated free T4/T3 and TSH-receptor antibodies; (2) toxic multinodular goiter, hot nodules on scan; (3) toxic adenoma; (4) thyroiditis, transient thyrotoxicosis with low radioiodine uptake; (5) exogenous hormone or iodine intake; (6) factitious thyroid hormone use.

Recommended investigations. First-line TSH, free T4, free T3; TSH-receptor antibodies (TRAb) to confirm Graves; thyroid ultrasound and radioiodine uptake scan when etiology is unclear; ECG for atrial fibrillation; CBC and LFTs at baseline before antithyroid therapy consultation.

Traceability note: any statement on antithyroid drug choice, dosing, or radioiodine must attach a retrieved guideline citation; otherwise the claim is flagged and suppressed.
